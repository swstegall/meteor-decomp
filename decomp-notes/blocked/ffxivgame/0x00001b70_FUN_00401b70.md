## Root-cause guess

MSVC's register allocator picks differently for the three
callee-saved values:

| value             | orig     | ours     |
|-------------------|----------|----------|
| caption           | EDI      | ESI      |
| text              | EBX      | EDI      |
| &g_dlg->timer_active | ESI (alive across both blocks) | EBX (block 1) → ESI (block 2) |
| result (after MB) | EDI (reuses caption) | EDI (reuses text) |

Both orig and ours shrink-wrap the third callee-saved register inside
the `if (g_dlg != 0)` arm (PUSH at 0x3b, POP before the final RET).
Both put `result` in EDI by coalescing with one of the message-box
arg registers. The difference is *which* arg register coalesces with
result:

- **orig**: result coalesces with caption (the value pushed 3rd-from-
  last for MessageBoxW). caption dies at PUSH EDI, then EDI is free
  for `MOV EDI, EAX` after the CALL.
- **ours**: result coalesces with text (the value pushed 2nd-from-last,
  closest to CALL). text dies at PUSH EDI, then EDI is free for
  `MOV EDI, EAX`. Tighter coalesce window.

Both are legitimate. The 7-byte structural difference at 0x86 is
because in ours, EBX (block-1 active) and ESI (block-2 active) are
different registers → MSVC emits an early POP EBX between the two
blocks. In orig, ESI is reused for both blocks so no intermediate POP.

The choice between "coalesce result with caption (gap)" vs "coalesce
result with text (tight)" appears to be an MSVC allocator tiebreaker
that I couldn't influence from the source side. Tried: declaration
order, const-qualifier, function-scope vs body-scope for active,
joint vs separate declarations of active+result, /O1 / /O2 / /Ox
flag variants. None tipped the choice.
